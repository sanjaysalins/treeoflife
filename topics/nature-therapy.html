Mindful Time in God’s Creation

Nature therapy (also called ecotherapy, forest bathing, or Shinrin-Yoku) is mindful immersion in natural environments to promote healing and well-being. It fits holistic health, which cares for the whole person: body, mind, and spirit. It also fits a biblical worldview that sees creation as God’s good gift for our flourishing.

1. The Biophilia Hypothesis

The Biophilia Hypothesis proposes that humans have an innate, genetically influenced tendency to seek connection with nature and other living things. The word means "love of life" (Greek bio = life, philia = love). It gives a foundational scientific explanation for why nature therapy, forest bathing, and a healthy relationship with the natural world promote holistic health.

2. What the Research Says

Decades of studies, especially from Japan (where Shinrin-Yoku began in the 1980s as preventive medicine), suggest real benefits:

Stress Reduction

Time in forests is linked with lower cortisol (the stress hormone), blood pressure, and pulse rate, and with more parasympathetic ("rest-and-digest") activity. A 2017 review of studies showed significant decreases in stress markers.

Mental Health

Research links nature time to less anxiety, depression, rumination (repetitive negative thinking), and negative emotion, and to better mood, self-compassion, and attention. A 90-minute nature walk has been shown to reduce activity in brain areas linked to rumination. Benefits appear in both clinical and general populations.

Immune & Physical Health

Trees, especially evergreens, release natural compounds called phytoncides. Exposure is linked with more natural killer (NK) cells, which help the body fight viruses and cancer, and the effect can last over a week. Nature time may also improve sleep, support heart health, and reduce inflammation.

Other Benefits

Research suggests help with ADHD symptoms in children, better recovery in hospital patients with a view of nature, and better overall well-being. Spending at least 120 minutes a week in nature is linked with better self-reported health.

This page is for education and is not medical advice. Please speak to your doctor about any health condition.

3. A Whole-Person Perspective

Forest bathing addresses the whole person: body (physical changes), mind (mood and thinking), and spirit (connection and awe). It works through several routes at once: sensory input, phytoncides, fewer urban stressors, and mindful presence.

4. Practical Applications

Guided Sessions

Begin with prayer and thanksgiving. Walk slowly, using your senses, while reflecting on Scripture such as Psalm 23 or "consider the lilies" (Matthew 6:28).

Discussion Prompts

How does time in creation reveal God’s character? In what ways has nature refreshed your soul?

Daily Practice

Aim for regular green-space time. Journal your observations alongside relevant verses, and combine them with breath prayers.

Accessibility

Local parks work well. Adapt the time and pace for all ages and abilities.

Remember: walk slowly, breathe deeply, and use all your senses: the sights, sounds, smells, and textures of God’s creation.

Nature therapy reminds us that the God who declared creation "very good" still uses it to restore us, in body, mind, and spirit. It is a gentle, evidence-supported invitation to rest in the Shepherd’s care.
